Harpers Ferry

Armory town where John Brown struck at slavery

Harpers Ferry, West Virginia, where the Potomac and Shenandoah Rivers meet

Harpers Ferry, at the meeting of the Potomac and Shenandoah Rivers, was home to a U.S. armory where interchangeable parts were first successfully used in manufacturing. In October 1859 the abolitionist John Brown seized the armory in a failed attempt to spark a slave uprising. During the Civil War it saw the largest surrender of Union troops of the war. After the war it became home to one of the earliest integrated schools in the country.

Why it matters

Harpers Ferry concentrates several stories of nineteenth-century America in one small town: industrial innovation, the violent struggle over slavery, the Civil War, and the beginnings of Black education after emancipation.

About the place

The town sits on steep ground at the junction of the Potomac and Shenandoah Rivers. Its preserved nineteenth-century streets and buildings are part of Harpers Ferry National Historical Park, which also studies the area’s archeological record going back millennia.

History

The National Park Service describes Harpers Ferry’s history as multilayered. The U.S. armory there pioneered interchangeable manufacture, and the town was reached by one of the first successful American railroads. On October 16, 1859, John Brown and twenty-one followers seized the armory, arsenal, and rifle factory; within thirty-six hours the raid was crushed and Brown captured by Marines under Robert E. Lee.

During the Civil War, Harpers Ferry was the site of the largest surrender of Federal troops during the war. After the war, freedpeople were educated in one of the earliest integrated schools in the country.
