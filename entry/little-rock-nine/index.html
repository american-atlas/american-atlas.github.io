The Little Rock Nine

The desegregation of Central High School, 1957

September 4–25, 1957 · Little Rock, Arkansas

In September 1957, nine Black students tried to enroll at all-white Central High School in Little Rock, Arkansas, under a court-ordered desegregation plan. Governor Orval Faubus sent the Arkansas National Guard to keep them out, and a white mob gathered outside. After weeks of standoff, President Dwight D. Eisenhower sent troops of the Army’s 101st Airborne Division to escort and protect the students, a dramatic use of federal power to enforce Brown v. Board of Education.

Why it matters

The Little Rock crisis was a defining test of Brown and of the federal government’s willingness to enforce it.

Historical background

Three years after the Supreme Court ruled in Brown that segregated schools were unconstitutional, Daisy Bates, president of the Arkansas NAACP, recruited nine students to enroll at Central High.

Causes and contributing factors

Governor Orval Faubus used state power to block the students and defy the federal courts.

What happened

On September 4, 1957, the first day of school, a white mob gathered in front of Central High, and Faubus deployed the Arkansas National Guard to bar the Black students. NAACP lawyers, including Thurgood Marshall, won a federal injunction against the governor. On September 23 the students entered through a side entrance with police escorts but were rushed home as the mob grew. Martin Luther King Jr. urged Eisenhower to take “a strong forthright stand.” Aware that Little Rock was becoming an international embarrassment, Eisenhower reluctantly sent the 101st Airborne; soldiers and federalized National Guardsmen protected the students for the rest of the school year.

Immediate consequences

In May 1958 Ernest Green became Central High’s first Black graduate; King attended the ceremony. That fall Faubus closed all of Little Rock’s public high schools rather than continue desegregation, but the courts soon ordered the schools reopened and desegregation resumed.

Long-term significance

Little Rock showed that the federal government would use force to uphold the Constitution against state defiance, and it made the nine students symbols of courage. Central High School is now a National Historic Site, and still an operating public high school.
