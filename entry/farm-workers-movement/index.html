The Farm Workers Movement

The Delano grape strike, the boycott, and the United Farm Workers

1962–1975 · California’s Central Valley and boycott centers nationwide

In the 1960s, Mexican American and Filipino farm workers in California organized to win union recognition, decent wages, and safe conditions. Their Delano grape strike, begun in 1965, and a nationwide consumer boycott of table grapes led by Cesar Chavez, Dolores Huerta, and Larry Itliong won the first major union contracts in California agriculture by 1970. The United Farm Workers became both a union and a civil rights movement.

Why it matters

The farm workers’ struggle joined labor organizing to the civil rights movement and made Chicano and Filipino workers visible in national politics.

Goals

Farm workers sought union recognition, higher wages (grape pickers averaged about 90 cents an hour in 1965), an end to child labor and dangerous conditions, protection from pesticides, and fair treatment. Chavez envisioned the union as both a labor organization and a civil rights movement for Chicano and Filipino workers.

Strategies

On September 8, 1965, Filipino workers of the AWOC, organized by Larry Itliong, struck nine farms near Delano. The NFWA voted to join on September 16. Strikers used roving pickets to persuade strikebreakers to leave the fields. Chavez called for a consumer boycott of non-union grapes, and volunteers organized boycott centers in cities across the country. In 1966 strikers marched nearly 340 miles to Sacramento; on Easter they announced that Schenley had signed a contract. Chavez fasted to draw attention to the cause.

Opposition

Growers used strikebreakers, and police sometimes harassed strikers. The Teamsters union offered growers a more conservative alternative, and the farm workers had to win union elections against them.

Achievements

By 1970 grape growers had signed contracts, and the UFW claimed 50,000 members, with a union hiring hall that ended favoritism by labor contractors, a health clinic and health plan, and higher wages. In 1975 California passed the Agricultural Labor Relations Act, the first U.S. law recognizing farm workers’ right to collective bargaining.

Unresolved questions

The National Labor Relations Act of 1935 excluded agricultural workers, leaving farm workers without the federal protections most other workers had.
