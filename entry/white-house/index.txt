23:T423,In March 1792 the commissioners of the new federal city announced a national design competition for the President’s House; Thomas Jefferson himself submitted a design, but in July the commissioners, with Washington’s approval, chose Hoban’s. The cornerstone was laid at a Masonic ceremony on October 13, 1792. Enslaved people worked on every stage of construction from 1792 to 1800 — quarrying stone, cutting timber, making bricks, and working as carpenters, sawyers, and laborers — alongside free white workers and craftsmen. Because the new capital had few residents, the commissioners hired enslaved people from slave owners in Maryland, Virginia, and the District of Columbia, and the owners collected their wages. Hoban himself owned enslaved carpenters — payrolls from 1794 to 1797 list four of them, Ben, Daniel, Harry, and Peter — and he grew rich building the new capital.
