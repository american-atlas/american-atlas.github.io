It appeared in recognizable form in the 1880s or 1890s, and by the early 1900s it was spreading across the South from Virginia to Texas. The Mississippi Delta, settled largely after the Civil War by Black workers recruited to clear land and work its plantations, was especially fertile ground. Its population was overwhelmingly African American, its plantation owners often tolerated juke joints, and musicians knew when plantations, levee camps, and logging camps paid their workers.
